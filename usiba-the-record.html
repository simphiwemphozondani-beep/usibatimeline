A Literary History:
The Record

Before the press, the record was spoken. From 1823 it was also printed. This is the line USIBA enters.

Before 1823

Oral tradition

Oral traditions

Before the press, literature lived in performance: izibongo, iintsomi, iingoma, proverbs, histories and other forms of oral knowledge carried across generations.

1823

Printing press

The Tyhume Press

A printing press arrives at Tyhume. The first printed isiXhosa emerges, establishing the foundations of a written literary culture.

1840s

Education / publishing

Lovedale

Lovedale grows into a centre of education, language, publishing and intellectual life in the Eastern Cape.

1867

Translation

Tiyo Soga

Soga's translation of The Pilgrim's Progress is published at Lovedale, extending the possibilities of isiXhosa in print.

1881

Newspaper

John Tengo Jabavu

Jabavu becomes editor of Isigidimi SamaXhosa, helping establish the newspaper as a space for African writing and public debate.

1884

Newspaper

Imvo Zabantsundu

Jabavu establishes Imvo Zabantsundu in King William's Town. The newspaper becomes a major platform for Black intellectual and literary life.

1906

Anthology

Zemk' Inkomo Magwalandini!

W. B. Rubusana gathers poetry, prose and intellectual writing in one of the early major anthologies of African literature.

1914

Novel

Ityala Lamawele

S. E. K. Mqhayi brings Xhosa law, custom and inherited knowledge into the form of the modern novel.

1920–1929

Poetry

Nontsizi Mgqwetho

Nontsizi Mgqwetho publishes a remarkable body of isiXhosa poetry in Umteteli wa Bantu.

1940s

Novel / scholarship

A. C. Jordan

Jordan extends the tradition through fiction, scholarship and African-language intellectual work.

1940s–1960s

Intellectual life

Fort Hare

Fort Hare becomes part of the Eastern Cape's wider literary and intellectual infrastructure.

1960

Memoir

Noni Jabavu

Noni Jabavu carries the Eastern Cape literary tradition into memoir and an international literary career.

1972

Archive

Literary archive

The National English Literary Museum is established in Makhanda, making the Eastern Cape a place not only of literary production, but of preservation.

2019

Archive

Amazwi

Amazwi expands its collecting mandate to all of South Africa's official languages, with an initial focus on isiXhosa.

2026

Literary platform

USIBA

USIBA enters this lineage: a publication-first literary platform rooted in the Eastern Cape.

2026

Literary journal

IMBEWU

IMBEWU begins the next record. New writing enters the archive.
